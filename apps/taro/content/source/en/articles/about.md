# About tarot and Taro

Taro is a reflective tarot journal. It uses the images and traditions of tarot as prompts for thinking about your life, your choices and your feelings. It does not predict the future, and nothing in the app tells you what is going to happen.

## A short history of tarot

Tarot began as a deck of playing cards. The earliest known decks were painted in northern Italy in the fifteenth century for a card game, and some of those games are still played in parts of Europe today. A tarot deck has four suits, like ordinary playing cards, plus a set of picture cards called trumps.

In the late eighteenth century, writers in France began to treat the cards as a source of symbolism and meaning rather than only as a game. Over the following century, many authors added their own ideas, and in the early twentieth century an influential English deck gave every card, including the numbered ones, an illustrated scene. Most modern decks, and the traditional card names used in Taro, follow that structure.

Today people use tarot in many ways: as art, as a creative tool, as a game, and as a way to reflect. Taro focuses on reflection.

## How the deck is organized

A tarot deck has 78 cards:

- **The Major Arcana**: 22 cards, from The Fool (0) to The World (21). They often stand for big themes and turning points, such as beginnings, balance, change and completion.
- **The Minor Arcana**: 56 cards in four suits of 14. Each suit has cards from Ace to Ten and four court cards: Page, Knight, Queen and King.
  - **Wands** (fire): energy, drive, creativity and ambition.
  - **Cups** (water): feelings, relationships and intuition.
  - **Swords** (air): thoughts, words, conflict and clarity.
  - **Pentacles** (earth): work, the body, resources and everyday life.

A card can appear **upright** or **reversed** (upside down). A reversed card often points to the same theme turned inward, blocked, delayed, or in need of a different approach. If you prefer to work only with upright cards, you can turn reversed cards off in Settings.

Taro's deck uses original art made for the app, and every card text was written for Taro.

## Using tarot for reflection

A tarot card doesn't know anything about you. What it can do is offer an image and a set of ideas that you hold up against your own situation. Often the most useful part of a reading is what you notice in yourself when you read it: what feels familiar, what you resist, what you had not thought about.

Some ways to get more from a reading:

- **Ask open questions.** "What can I learn from this situation?" gives you more to work with than "Will this work out?". Questions that start with "what" or "how" usually work well.
- **Stay with the question you care about.** Drawing again and again for the same question tends to blur things rather than clarify them.
- **Read the cards together.** In a spread, each position gives a card a role, such as "Past" or "Challenge". Notice how the cards relate to one another.
- **Treat "future" and "outcome" as a direction, not fate.** These positions describe where things may be heading if nothing changes. Your choices can change them.
- **Write it down.** Your journal keeps every reading and daily card with your notes. Over time, the Patterns view shows which cards and suits come up most in your draws. These are patterns in what you drew, not predictions.

## The daily card and Learn

Your daily card is free, needs no internet connection and uses no AI. It shows the card's name, keywords, a short meaning and a reflection question written for Taro.

Learn contains the full text for all 78 cards, upright and reversed, with notes on relationships, work and personal growth, plus a guide to each spread and its positions. Learn works completely offline.

## How AI readings work

When you ask for an AI reading, Taro sends four things to its server: your question (if you wrote one), the spread, the cards you drew with their orientation, and your app language. The server asks OpenAI's GPT models to write an interpretation of the whole spread. The AI is given Taro's own card names, keywords and short meanings and a description of each position, and it is instructed to write in a reflective voice, to refer to the cards you drew, and to avoid predictions and advice.

A few things to know about privacy:

- Taro never sends your name, email address or advertising ID with a reading.
- Your question is not stored on Taro's server.
- The finished reading is kept on the server, encrypted, only until your phone has received it (at most 7 days), and is then deleted.
- If you choose to report a reading, your question and that reading are kept for 90 days so they can be reviewed.
- OpenAI does not use this data to train its models.
- Your full history, questions and notes stay on your device.

AI readings need your permission. You can allow or turn off AI data sharing at any time in **Settings → Privacy & data → AI readings**. Without it, you can still use the daily card, Learn and your journal, and you can choose a **Classic reading**: the same draw, with the authored meaning of each card in its position, created on your phone without AI.

## What AI readings can't do

AI readings are generated text. They can be thoughtful and useful, but they have real limits:

- **They can be wrong or unexpected.** The AI can misread a situation, repeat itself, or say something that doesn't fit. Take what is useful and leave the rest.
- **They don't know you.** The AI only sees your question and the cards. It has no memory of your past readings and no knowledge of your life.
- **They can't see the future.** No reading can tell you what will occur, and Taro's readings never try to.
- **They don't answer some questions.** Taro doesn't give readings on health, pregnancy, death, legal matters, money decisions or gambling. When a question falls into one of these areas, the app explains why and suggests a way to rephrase it, and no reading is used. For those topics, please talk to a qualified professional.
- **They are not a substitute for support.** If you are going through something painful, talking with someone you trust can help. If you are thinking about harming yourself or someone else, please reach out now: **Settings → Help → Support lines** lists helplines for your region.

If a reading seems harmful or inappropriate, use **Report this reading** in the reading's menu. Reports help improve Taro.
